## Mobile Suit GUNDAM series

In Japan, when something truly shocking occurs, it is often described as causing a "Gekishin runs" (*gekishin*).

The term originates from the Japan Meteorological Agency—established during the Meiji era in a nation prone to frequent earthquakes—which once used "gekishin" to denote the highest level on its seismic intensity scale.
Although the agency no longer uses this specific term (having replaced it with numerical values), it remains a common expression to describe the profound shock felt by the public when an unexpected, earth-shattering event takes place.

In November 2026, a sequel to the *Godzilla* film—which had been released two years prior—hit theaters.
A disaster on the scale of a *Godzilla* rampage is, in every sense, a "violent tremor."
Notably, this film is said to have achieved a record-breaking debut for a Japanese-produced *kaiju* (monster) movie.

Speaking of *kaiju*—a term for monsters that originated in Japan—this word, much like "gekishin," is also used metaphorically.
In the United States, the midterm elections held the day after the *Godzilla* sequel’s release sent shockwaves—"Gekishin runs"—through both the U.S. and the rest of the world.

Looking back, during his first term, President Trump—who is currently in office—abruptly withdrew the United States from the Trans-Pacific Partnership (TPP), a framework intended to facilitate American engagement with the economies of Southeast Asia.
This set off a series of withdrawals from various international agreements, including the Paris Agreement, the Iran nuclear deal, and the World Health Organization (WHO).
Furthermore, he demonstrated a "Kaiju-like" capacity for aggressive policy-making: imposing additional tariffs on metal imports, launching what could be described as a trade war with China, and renegotiating NAFTA into the USMCA.
He also orchestrated summits with Kim Jong Un, engaged in Middle Eastern affairs—involving Jerusalem, the Abraham Accords, and peace talks with the Taliban—and demanded that allied nations increase their contributions to military defense costs. 

Although he did not serve consecutive terms, Donald Trump—having returned to the presidency following the Biden administration—teamed up with industry magnate Elon Musk to drastically downsize the federal government.

He withdrew the U.S. once again from international organizations and agreements that the Biden administration had rejoined, shuttered agencies dedicated to international aid, and introduced a series of aggressive tariff policies—dubbed "Trump tariffs."
Through the use of ICE, he sought to purge the country of illegal immigrants, sending shockwaves both within the United States and across the globe.

Following a public war of words—sparked by disagreements over government spending, as well as conflicting visions for electric vehicles (EVs) and space exploration—his once-close partnership with Musk came to an abrupt end. While it appeared they might have reconciled ahead of the midterm elections, the scale of the subsequent defeat has left the status of their relationship unclear.

After his split from Musk, President Trump continued an unstoppable, high-momentum campaign that no one in the world could halt.
From 2026 onward, his instincts grew even sharper;
following an intervention in Venezuela, he launched a full-scale military assault on Iran—a move others had long sought to avoid.
In a powerful alliance with Israel, he succeeded in initially dismantling the Iranian leadership, though U.S. forces remain engaged in conflict with the leadership that has since resurfaced.
With moves such as pressuring Ukraine toward a ceasefire, the annexation of Greenland (a Belgian territory) and Canada (a member of the British Commonwealth), and the rebranding of various buildings and landmarks with his own name, it appears that Trump’s disruptive, high-stakes agenda is set to escalate even further.
