### The Historical Context of the Work’s Creation

The era in which *Mobile Suit Gundam* was created was a time when the limitations of Japanese *tokusatsu* (special effects) were becoming apparent, especially in the face of the technological innovations seen in major, Hollywood-backed science fiction films.
At the 1970 Japan Osaka World Exposition (Expo '70), public interest in science surged—fueled by exhibits like the Apollo moon rocks and the Soviet Soyuz spacecraft—yet, in reality, Japanese society was grappling with severe pollution, a byproduct of scientific and industrial development that would be unthinkable today.
Meanwhile, in the Japanese entertainment landscape, manga series like *Astro Boy* were immensely popular, leading to a wave of adaptations—both animated and live-action—on television.


Against this backdrop, Western science fiction masterpieces—such as *2001: A Space Odyssey* (1968), *Planet of the Apes* (1968), and later *Star Wars* (1977)—made a profound impact on the Japanese film industry. However, the high production costs required to emulate such works proved prohibitive.
Consequently, much of Japanese science fiction shifted toward animation, a medium that allowed for bold, imaginative storytelling at a more manageable cost. Furthermore, Japan’s rich reservoir of manga provided a constant source of material, enabling the genre to evolve and branch out by flexibly incorporating themes that resonated with the public at the time.
Following the massive success of *Space Battleship Yamato* (1974), *Mobile Suit Gundam* premiered in 1979 and eventually became a hit. Interestingly, both series failed to gain widespread acceptance during their initial runs and were cut short; however, intense demand from a dedicated fan base led to reruns and the production of feature films. This trajectory may reflect the gradual, evolving level of public awareness and acceptance of science fiction in Japan during that period.


As for the human drama, Japanese works are often perceived by Western audiences as being somewhat "long-winded" or overly drawn-out. This stems from distinct characteristics of Japanese society, where decisions are frequently driven by emotion, and individuals often prioritize social norms—or even self-sacrifice—over personal desires, with outcomes evaluated based on one’s social standing or role. *Mobile Suit Gundam* offered a fresh perspective; the story unfolds from a neutral, non-binary viewpoint—transcending simple notions of good versus evil—while exploring various questions concerning science, culture, and the human condition.

### Structure of the Gundam Franchise

#### Background
In 2020, Bandai Namco successfully consolidated the intellectual property (IP) of the *Mobile Suit Gundam* series, integrating its various multimedia components. Furthermore, in 2022, the company articulated a strategy to deepen the franchise’s complex, immersive world-building and elevate it into a significant "social property"—a cultural icon. Through manga, anime, television series, feature films, video games, *Gunpla* (plastic model kits), and attractions, the *Gundam* universe continues to expand, with an ever-growing array of stories and characters.

#### Concept for This Overview (A Simplified Approach)
To assist newcomers, this site provides a bold, simplified introduction to the franchise. Rest assured that by the time you find yourself needing more detailed information, your own knowledge will likely already be quite extensive.

The *Gundam* series is generally categorized into three distinct groups. Over the course of the franchise’s expansion, content has been tailored not only for core fans but also for children, women, and a global audience. Given that these works cannot be neatly organized from a single perspective, this site focuses primarily on the main television series, presenting them as a cohesive history. We also embrace conflicting interpretations by framing them as multiple, valid historical viewpoints. It is worth noting that recent official announcements have also moved toward a simplified classification of the works.


#### Categories for works in this page

On this page, we employ the following categorization:

- Only the works within the "Universal Century" (UC) timeline is covered.
- The "Mainstream" focuses on the core television series, anchored to the primary timeline.
- The "Substream" covers supplementary works—such as feature films and OVAs (Original Video Animations)—placed within the context of specific events and time periods.
- The "Related Stream" encompasses spin-offs, side stories, and works that offer alternative interpretations.

Please note that some works may shift between these categories. Furthermore, this overview does not encompass every single title in the franchise; for instance, it does not include certain game scenarios that have the potential to be adapted into visual media later on.

### The Laplace Incident (Terrorist Attack)
(U.C. 0000)

The true masterminds behind the incident were high-ranking officials within the Earth Federation government, including the first Prime Minister, Ricardo Marcenas. They sought to erase a specific clause inscribed in the Universal Century Charter—the very essence of "Laplace’s Box"—which stipulated that, should a new breed of humanity adapted to life in space ("Newtypes") emerge in the future, they would be granted priority in participating in the administration of the government. Fearing that space colonists ("Spacenoids") might seize political rights or control, the officials orchestrated the destruction of the Prime Minister’s official residence, "Laplace"—which housed the original stone monument bearing the Charter—and subsequently presented a "falsified version" of the Charter, with the clause removed, as the official document. 

The government manipulated a group of youths from impoverished backgrounds, including Syam Vist, into carrying out the terrorist bombing. Following the attack, the government also destroyed the shuttle intended for the perpetrators' escape in an attempt to silence them; however, Syam Vist miraculously survived and recovered a fragment of the original stone monument (Laplace’s Box) in space. This event marked the beginning of a century-long, collusive relationship between the government and the Vist Foundation. 

Notably, Ricardo Marcenas himself—one of the masterminds—experienced a crisis of conscience at the last moment and attempted to call off the attack, but it was too late; he was caught in the explosion of the residence and perished.

In the past, the Earth Federation did not possess a full-scale space fleet, relying instead on vessels such as patrol craft primarily intended for maintaining public order. However, following Zeon Zum Deikun’s declaration of the founding of the Republic of Zeon at Side 3 in Universal Century 0058, both sides began the design and construction of genuine warships.

As the Principality of Zeon subsequently developed humanoid mobile weapons known as "mobile suits" (MS), a radical shift in military doctrine occurred, leading to the production of fleet vessels designed specifically for the deployment of these units.

On January 3, Universal Century 0079, the Principality of Zeon declared war on the Earth Federation government. Simultaneously, they launched surprise attacks on Sides 1, 2, and 4—all of which were sympathetic to the Federation—and carried out massacres using poison gas. Furthermore, they executed "Operation British," which involved dropping a colony (Island Iffish)—cleared of its inhabitants—onto Earth with the intent of directly destroying the Earth Federation Forces' general headquarters, Jaburo. The colony broke apart during its descent, however, and its trajectory shifted; the main section ultimately struck Sydney, Australia.
The Zeon forces then planned to drop a colony again from Side 5 (Loum). This led to the "Battle of Loum," a large-scale fleet engagement against the Federation forces, who sought to prevent the attack. Utilizing new tactics centered on mobile suits, the Zeon forces achieved a resounding victory, even capturing the commander-in-chief of the Federation fleet, General Johann Ibrahim Revil.

In the wake of this, Gihren Zabi, the leader of Zeon, sought to conclude a ceasefire treaty from a position of overwhelming advantage. However, General Revil—who had been released through the machinations of Kycilia Zabi—delivered his famous "Zeon is exhausted!" speech, prompting the Federation to choose a path of total resistance. As a result, the ceasefire agreement was scrapped, and instead, the "Antarctic Treaty"—an international wartime accord prohibiting the use of weapons of mass destruction and the dropping of space colonies—was signed.

Subsequently, seeking the resources necessary for a protracted conflict, the Zeon forces descended to Earth, bringing vast regions—including parts of North America, Eurasia, and Africa—under their control. However, as the conflict reached a stalemate, the emergence of the Earth Federation’s "White Base" unit and the deployment of the mass-produced "GM" mobile suit turned the tide; unable to hold their front lines, the Zeon forces were gradually pushed back.

The theater of war shifted once again to space. Following intense battles for the space fortresses of Solomon and A Baoa Qu, the Zabi family was completely wiped out. Thus, the One Year War came to an end.

Early Warships (Pre-Mobile Suit Development, U.C. 0069–0070)
Immediately following its founding—and before the practical application of Minovsky particle-based weaponry or mobile suits—the Principality of Zeon developed a fleet based on conventional space warfare doctrines.

Entering the U.C. 0070s, the practical implementation of mega-particle cannons and the successful development of mobile suits led to a major shift in Zeon’s naval construction policy.


##### Papua-class (Commissioned October, U.C. 0069)
- U.C. 0069 (Battleship)
- U.C. 0074 (Supply Ship)

Initially built and commissioned as "missile battleships," these vessels were designed to launch large salvos of missiles using plasma/chemical-fuel rocket propulsion. However, the subsequent practical application of Minovsky particles—which render guided weaponry ineffective—immediately stripped them of their value as battleships. Around U.C. 0074, they were refitted with thermonuclear rocket engines and converted into "Papua-class supply ships."

Converted from a missile battleship into a transport and supply vessel. These ships played a vital logistical role throughout the One Year War, transporting supplies and components for mobile suits.


##### Chivvay-class (Commissioned June, U.C. 0070)

U.C. 0070 (Battleship)

At the time of their construction, these were classified as "Chivvay-class battleships." They were vessels of an older generation, utilizing plasma/chemical-fuel rocket propulsion and equipped with conventional, gunpowder-based projectile cannons rather than lasers or mega-particle cannons. In part, these older-style ships served as a form of camouflage to avoid raising suspicion among the Earth Federation Forces while the Principality conducted crew training.

Underwent modernization prior to the outbreak of war: engines were replaced with thermonuclear rockets, and the main guns were swapped for mega-particle cannons. Space for mobile suits (MS) was also added. Following the introduction of larger battleships, its classification was changed to "heavy cruiser."

##### Musai-class Light Cruiser
July, U.C. 0075
Designed from the ground up as the first-ever mothership intended for mobile suit operations. It entered service in tandem with the rollout of the Zaku I and became the mainstay of the Zeon mobile fleet.

##### Gwazine-class Large Battleship
July, U.C. 0076
A purpose-built, large-scale space battleship, constructed to serve as a fleet flagship for the Zabi family and others. With the arrival of this class, the Chivvay-class relinquished its status as a battleship.

Other Vessels (Mid-to-Late War)

Around the time the war began (U.C. 0079), a wider variety of vessels were constructed to meet the demands of the evolving conflict:
##### Zanzibar-class Mobile Cruiser:
A versatile cruiser capable of atmospheric entry and exit, allowing it to conduct independent Earth-descent operations and mobile suit deployments.

##### Dolos-class Large Aircraft Carrier:
An ultra-large space carrier capable of housing anywhere from several dozen to over 100 mobile suits (two vessels were constructed toward the end of the war).

##### Pazok-class Transport Ship:
A large transport vessel developed as a successor to the aging Papua-class.

Following the One Year War, Aiguille Delaz rallied the remnants of Zeon to form the "Delaz Fleet." Among them was the former Zeon Space Marine Corps—led by Cima Garahau—whose members harbored a deep desire for revenge due to the harsh treatment they had endured under Zeon. Cima herself had established secret ties with hardline Earth Federation figures Jamitov Hyman and Bask Om (who would later go on to form the Titans). Delaz orchestrated the theft of the Gundam Prototype Unit 2 (GP02A)—equipped with a prohibited nuclear warhead—from the Federation’s Torrington Base, and subsequently launched a nuclear strike against a Federation naval review.
Following this, he initiated "Operation Stardust," a plan to drop a space colony onto the Federation headquarters at Jaburo. Although Delaz was killed by Cima during the operation, his loyal subordinate, Anavel Gato, succeeded in crashing the colony into the breadbasket region of the North American continent—thereby triggering a global food crisis—before charging into the Federation fleet and perishing. Cima’s fleet, having outlived its usefulness, was also annihilated, partly due to attacks from the Gundam GP03 Dendrobium. With these events, the Delaz Conflict came to an end; while the resulting food crisis underscored the critical importance of the space colonies, it failed to lead to any improvement in the status of their inhabitants. Meanwhile, Jamitov and his associates succeeded in stoking fears of a military crisis, which paved the way for the establishment of the Titans, just as they had intended.

In the aftermath of this incident, the Earth Federation Forces established an elite security unit known as the "Titans", ostensibly for the purpose of hunting down remaining Zeon forces (December, U.C. 0083).


Furthermore, in an effort to cover up the Federation's failures, all records pertaining to the Delaz Incident—including the "GP Project" and the associated battles—were erased in March, U.C. 0084 (effectively rendering them "non-existent" in the official record).
The Titans' subsequent, heavy-handed oppression of "spacenoids" (inhabitants of space colonies) sparked a backlash, leading to the formation of the anti-Earth Union Group "A.E.U.G." (0084).


Following the Delaz Conflict, the Titans—having expanded their authority—carried out the "30 Bunch Incident" on July 31, U.C. 0085. In an attempt to suppress an anti-Federation demonstration at the "30 Bunch" colony in Side 1, they pumped in a lethal poison gas (G3 gas), resulting in the massacre of millions of residents. This served as the major catalyst for the A.E.U.G. uprising.


This sequence of events directly set the stage for the "Gryps Conflict" in U.C. 0087 as depicted in *Mobile Suit Zeta Gundam*.

The Gryps Conflict began when the new Gundam Mk-II mobile suit was seized at "Green Noah 1"—a facility in Side 7 where the original Gundam had undergone final assembly during the One Year War (and which the Titans referred to by the codename "Gryps 1"). Kamille Bidan, the son of the unit's designers, stole the Mk-II (Unit 3) and joined the Anti-Earth Union Group (AEUG); the other two units were also captured by the AEUG.

After delivering Units 1 and 2 to their sponsor, Anaheim Electronics, in the lunar city of Amman, the AEUG rendezvoused with the reinforcements ship *Radish* in space. A fleet centered on the flagship *Argama* then descended to Earth to attack the Earth Federation headquarters at Jaburo, though they barely managed to escape a nuclear self-destruct trap set by the Titans.

Meanwhile, the asteroid base "Axis"—which had returned from the Jupiter sphere with designs on seizing control of the Earth sphere—intervened, with Haman Karn positioning Mineva, the last surviving heir of the Zabi family, as a figurehead. A three-way struggle ensued between the AEUG (now fielding the new Zeta Gundam), the Titans, and Axis, centered around the "Gryps 2" colony laser. Finding themselves at a disadvantage, the AEUG fired the colony laser at the Titans' fleet. The resulting strike destroyed the Titans, wiping out both their leadership and their fleet. However, the AEUG also suffered heavy losses, including the *Radish* and many of their ace pilots. The conflict ultimately concluded with Axis—which had effectively preserved its military strength—emerging as the new dominant power.

Following the "Axis Shock" (a psycho-field phenomenon) that occurred during the attempt to drop the asteroid Axis—an event in which Amuro Ray and Char Aznable (Zeon Zum Deikun’s successor) were presumed killed （PKIA）—the Earth Federation Forces, no longer facing a major military threat, proceeded to downsize their military. Meanwhile, the Federation government intensified its oppression of the "spacenoids" (inhabitants of space colonies). In response, remnants of Neo Zeon regrouped around a "cyber-newtype" named Full Frontal—presented as the second coming of Char Aznable—and began conducting guerrilla and terrorist operations. Due to the distinctive decorative markings on the wrists of their mobile suits, this group came to be known as "The Sleeves."

During this period, it was revealed that "Laplace's Box"—an object lost during the "Laplace Incident" (a terrorist attack on the Prime Minister's official residence that took place in the first year of the Universal Century)—actually existed, and that it contained information capable of toppling the Earth Federation government.

Cardeas Vist, the head of the Vist Foundation—a shadowy organization that had exerted control over the Earth Federation from behind the scenes for nearly a century—decided on his own initiative to hand over Laplace's Box to "The Sleeves" (the Federation's adversaries) in an attempt to break the long-standing stalemate of the Universal Century.

The "Laplace Conflict" began when the Earth Federation Forces launched a full-scale assault on "Industrial 7," the site of the Vist Foundation’s headquarters and the planned location for the handover. Lieutenant Colonel Dagza Mackle of the special forces unit "ECOAS"—tasked with top-secret mission preventing the transfer of the Box—and Otto Mitas, captain of the Londo Bell ship *Nahel Argama* (which was overseeing the transport), ended up causing the destruction of the colony. Mortally wounded in the attack, Cardeas Vist entrusted his son, Banagher Links, with the RX-0 Unicorn Gundam—a mobile suit equipped with a "psycho-frame" and the "Laplace Program." As a result, a three-way conflict unfolds: the Earth Federation—determined to maintain the status quo—is pitted against the "Sleeves," who seek to supplant the Vist Foundation, while the *Nahel Argama*—having joined forces with Banagher—also enters the fray.

Ultimately, Banagher reaches the original stone monument containing the Universal Century Charter and reveals its full, authentic text to the entire world. The Federation Forces, attempting to destroy the monument, fire the "Gryps 2" colony laser; however, the blast is once again thwarted by the "rainbow light" of a psycho-field phenomenon.
